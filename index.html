01 / Why it matters

Evaluate what agents respond to, not only what they decide.

A simulated household can make the right choice for the wrong reason. If an agent's decision looks sensible but ignores the warning it was given, or shifts because of details that should not matter, the simulation cannot be trusted to inform emergency planning.

RQ 2.1

Geographic coherence

Do agents make geographically and temporally coherent decisions within reconstructed wildfire conditions?

RQ 2.2

Behavioral cue response

Do controlled changes in hazard, warnings, social information, and transportation produce theoretically defensible decision changes?

RQ 2.3

Grounding & robustness

Does geographic grounding improve response consistency under changing or held-out hazard conditions?

02 / Approach

Hold everything constant. Change one cue.

Each synthetic household scenario is built from public data on the January 2025 Eaton Fire: fire perimeters, evacuation zones, the road network, and census-based household characteristics. The same scenario is then run in pairs that differ in exactly one decision-relevant cue.

The expected direction of every change is written down before any model is run, using the Protective Action Decision Model from hazards research.

A decision can be geographically feasible without being a valid model of human behavior. This study measures feasibility and consistency with theory; it does not claim to predict what real Altadena households did.
